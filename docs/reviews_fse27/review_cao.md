# FSE 2027 Research Papers — Review

**Paper:** *When Is a Difference a Defect? Differential Validation of C-to-Rust Translations*
**Reviewer persona:** Jialun Cao (HKUST → Imperial College London), FSE 2027 Research Papers PC.
**Rubric:** my PC profile at `docs/reviews_fse27/pc_cao.md`, plus How2Bench where its criteria apply to an evaluation-heavy empirical paper.
**Scope note from the authors, respected here:** no comments on citation formatting, LaTeX, grammar, wording, or sentence-level style. Content only.

---

## 1. Paper summary

The paper argues that differential testing is not a free oracle for validating an already-produced C-to-Rust translation, because four obligations are usually left implicit: which C and Rust functions correspond, how one logical input is realized across two possibly reshaped representations, which observable state carries the functional contract, and whether an observed disagreement is attributable to the translation rather than to undefined behavior in the C reference, the harness, or instrumentation. The authors build a validator that makes each obligation explicit: a name-independent structural matcher with a two-sided abstention margin; a deterministic, LLM-free *HarnessPlan* that derives a canonical C-shaped input from the C definition and records supported adaptations to the Rust interface (or records a construction failure); Rust-only coverage-guided discovery followed by paired replay through a fixed comparison ladder; and a side-isolated confirmation funnel (C-only ASan + full UBSan, combined replay, Rust-only replay with and without sanitizers) that separates reference-side UB, instrumentation artifacts, non-reproduction, and out-of-contract executions before clustering and manual source-level triage. Evaluated over a 10-application × 6-system matrix, the validator confirms 36 defects in 15 artifacts from the five restructuring systems, 21 of which terminate normally and therefore give Rust-only crash fuzzing no signal, with zero confirmed defects in the ten mechanical c2rust controls; the defects are sorted into seven root-cause families, campaign reach is reported over 37 artifacts, released FLOURINE and RustAssure artifacts are run on the frozen positive corpus (14/16 and 5/11 of completed analyses), and five components are individually reduced on fixed evidence sets.

## 2. Overall decision

**Decision: Weak Reject.**
**Reviewer expertise: Knowledgeable.** (Expert on evaluation and benchmark methodology for code-LLM and code-translation systems; knowledgeable on C-to-Rust translation, differential testing, and coverage-guided fuzzing; *not* an expert on sanitizer internals, so I take the paper's UBSan/ASan claims at the level of their stated configuration.)
**Confidence: 4/5.** I read every section and every table, and I verified the funnel arithmetic and the defect accounting against the archived data the figure captions point to.

This is a careful, unusually honest paper whose numbers I could actually check, and most of them check out. I am not recommending rejection because the work is weak; I am recommending it because the paper never measures the one property its entire argument rests on — the discriminative power of its own oracle, in either direction — and because two of its headline numbers (the defect count's unit and the "15 of 25" denominator) do not survive the arithmetic in the paper's own tables. Both are fixable, and a revision that fixes them would move me up.

## 3. Strengths

**S1. The framing is normative and correct, and the paper resists the easy overclaim (§1, §Discussion).** The thesis — "the challenge is not merely to compare two executions, but to determine when their disagreement constitutes valid evidence of a translation defect" (§1) — is the right thesis, and the paper repeatedly refuses to convert it into a stronger claim than it earned: "Sanitizer-clean execution is therefore supporting evidence, not proof that the C reference is defined" (§1); "``Clean C'' means that no configured check fired, not that defined behavior has been proved" (Table 3 caption, Fig. 2 caption, §Discussion). In a literature where "our tool found N bugs" is the default register, this discipline is worth a lot.

**S2. The confirmation funnel is an actual attribution mechanism, not a filter with a nice name (§4.5, Table 3, Fig. 2).** Separating C-only ASan+UBSan, combined replay, Rust-only replay *with and without* ASan, and independent per-side timeouts is the right decomposition, and the paper states the one thing most such pipelines omit: the in-loop minimal-UBSan check "serves only as an inexpensive noise filter; the isolated runs perform attribution" (§4.5). The component row showing that in-loop UBSan admits a urlparser heap overflow that isolated ASan+UBSan correctly attributes to C (Table 8) is precisely the evidence that justifies the extra cost.

**S3. The funnel numbers are backed by the archived data.** I reconstructed them from `results/rq3_coverage/*/*/confirm_sample/summary.json`: across the five restructuring systems the per-boundary samples sum to 14,470 adjudicated records from 176,507 candidates; subtracting `not_reproducible` gives 13,515; `confirmed_divergence` + `confirmed_termination` sums to exactly 1,931; and the 3,365 "clean C" figure is reproducible as 3,116 clean-C records + 248 inconclusive + the one Laertes record the caption flags as having no final verdict. Every one of these matches §5.2 to the digit. Very few papers I review survive this check.

**S4. A real negative control, with a genuinely strong result the paper under-reports.** Ten mechanical c2rust artifacts run through the identical pipeline, and the paper correctly refuses to over-read it: "This use is an operational negative control, not an assumption that C2Rust is universally semantics preserving" (§4.3). The archived data is stronger than the sentence in §5.2: of 8,962 adjudicated control candidates, **zero** reach `confirmed_divergence` or `confirmed_termination`; all are UB-associated, instrument-only, not-reproducible, or inconclusive. See W5/Q6 — you should be reporting that number.

**S5. The motivating examples do the work they are asked to do (§2, Fig. 1, Table 1).** Three real artifacts, three different blind spots, and a signal table whose rows are exactly the acceptance conditions a reader would otherwise assume are sufficient. The zlib `state->offset > copy` → `state as usize > copy` case in §5.3 is the best single piece of evidence in the paper: a one-token rewrite, a precisely stated consequence ("compares the heap address of the entire state object with a small byte count"), and a counted manifestation (169 output-length divergences plus four null-window dereferences).

**S6. Honest negative and self-undercutting results are in the body, not an appendix.** The matcher "misses PtrTrans's renamed qsort API boundary, although a name-preserved internal boundary still exposes the same unique defect" (§5.6, Table 8) — i.e., the headline matcher changed the defect count by zero. Tulip's 0.554 precision is reported and explained mechanistically ("Its 213 functions collapse to only 103 static fingerprints", §5.1) rather than excused. Laertes' 64.9% Tulip reach is kept as the reported result while the flattering 92.0% decomposition is labeled a diagnostic (§5.4). This is the right instinct.

**S7. Abstention and construction failure are first-class outcomes, not silent losses (§4.2, §4.3, Table 6).** "If a required extent or adaptation cannot be derived, the plan records the construction failure rather than inserting a guessed constant" (§4.2), and the M/P/B columns make the attrition visible: 4,222 matched → 1,854 planned → 1,662 built → 1,483 with a coverage export. I checked these sums against Table 6 and they are exact. Keeping the un-deleted denominator ("We retain the translation's complete code map as the denominator rather than deleting code blocked by such a failure", §5.4) is the correct and unflattering choice.

**S8. The reach attribution is a real analysis, not a disclaimer.** Construction-limited / translation-limited / reference-limited / domain-limited is a genuine decomposition, and the cJSON × PtrTrans diagnosis — `cJSON_New_Item` is an untranslated stub returning `None` on all 18 direct-constructor witnesses, so "adding an `Option<&mut T>` adapter or running longer could not unlock the 64 object-taking boundaries" (§5.4) — is exactly how a low number should be explained: by ruling out the explanations that would be the reviewer's first two questions.

**S9. The baseline funnel separates applicability from sensitivity (Table 7, §5.5).** Reporting S/A/C/R/D with unsupported inputs, analysis failures, and compilation failures as distinct columns, and stating plainly that VERT's outcome "is an applicability boundary for our fixed-artifact question, not a semantic miss," is the fair way to report on released research artifacts. The columns sum to 36 for both FLOURINE and RustAssure; I checked.

**S10. The coverage denominator is defended with a measurement, not an argument (§5.4).** "Function and region denominators come directly from each translation's own LLVM-instrumented archive objects… For 11 artifacts initially measured through a linked test or denominator binary, rebuilding the universe from the archive objects reproduced the same in-scope functions and region counts." A provenance change verified to be a no-op, reported as such, is exactly the kind of check I want to see and almost never do.

## 4. Weaknesses, ranked by how much they drive my decision

### W1 (decisive). The paper never measures the discriminative power of its own oracle — in either direction.
**Where.** §5.2: "These reductions are candidate accounting, not an estimated false-positive rate, because confirmation caps each boundary's sample at 200." Fig. 2 caption: "the sample does not estimate a corpus-wide false-positive rate." §Discussion/Component checks: "it does not estimate population-level recall or precision, and the confirmed-defect corpus is not treated as exhaustive." There is no seeded-defect, mutation, or fault-injection experiment anywhere in the paper.

**Why this decides it for me.** The paper's own central claim is that passing a check is not the same as the check being able to discriminate; that is the thesis of §1 and the reason §4.5 exists. A validator that makes that argument must then submit to it. As written, I am told the pipeline found 36 defects and rejected a lot of other things, and I have no quantity anywhere that tells me (a) what fraction of *real* translation defects this oracle would catch, or (b) what fraction of its confirmed divergences are not defects. Both are measurable and neither is expensive. The paper even removes the one number that would partially answer (b): the funnel shows 1,931 confirmed divergence records collapsing to 36 defects, and describes the collapse purely as merging — "Source-level triage merges repeated inputs, boundaries, and symptoms caused by the same faulty rewrite into the 36 defects" — yet §4.5 says promotion also *rejects* candidates ("a correspondence that does not survive source-level review"). How many of the 1,931 were rejected rather than merged is never stated. That number is the paper's own measured false-positive rate of the automated stages, and it is missing.

**What would resolve it.** (i) A recall measurement: inject a stratified set of representative faulty rewrites (one per taxonomy family) into the ten c2rust control artifacts — you already have the family taxonomy and the artifacts — and report detection at the fixed budget, per family. This is the direct analogue of mutation-based test-adequacy measurement and it is the standard I hold my own work to. (ii) A precision measurement: report how many of the 1,931 confirmed records were manually reviewed, how many clusters were promoted, and how many were rejected at triage and why. (iii) Report the control's funnel (see W5).

### W2 (decisive). The defect-counting unit inflates the headline, and the paper knows it without quantifying it.
**Where.** Abstract and §1: "confirms 36 distinct translation defects"; §5.2: "20 occur in C2SaferRust, 7 in Laertes, four each in PtrTrans and CROWN, and 1 in SACTOR"; §Discussion: "The 36 instances are not 36 independent mechanisms." §5.2 also says the defects "range from a 30-line qsort to the bzip2 and optipng codecs; they are therefore not confined to one program scale or translation strategy."

**Why it matters to me.** "36 distinct defects" is the paper's primary quantitative claim and it is counted at the granularity of a faulty rewrite *site*, which is defensible, but the paper never reports the granularity a reader will actually infer — distinct translator-level flaws. From the manifest the tables are generated from: all seven Laertes defects are instances of one pattern (a `laertes_init_*` initializer emitted and never called) at seven different static-data sites across four applications; four of the seven byte-string-narrowing defects are the same `to_str().unwrap()` idiom at four sites. So at least eleven of the 36 collapse to two translator-level root causes. Worse for the cross-system comparison: Table 2's own cells show **10 of the 36 come from a single artifact** (optipng × C2SaferRust, "4C + 6S"), and 21 of 36 from just two applications (optipng 13, bzip2 8). The sentence quoted above actively reads against that concentration. And the per-system counts are not normalized by opportunity: C2SaferRust was compared on six artifacts and SACTOR on two (with seven compile failures), so "20 vs. 1" is not a statement about the systems, it is largely a statement about how many artifacts each system produced.

**What would resolve it.** Report the count at both granularities in the abstract and §5.2 ("36 faulty rewrite sites, corresponding to N distinct translator-level root causes"); add a per-artifact defect distribution so the concentration is visible; and either normalize per-system counts by compared artifacts / matched boundaries, or drop the per-system comparison to a descriptive note and say explicitly that the corpus does not support ranking the systems.

### W3 (decisive). Denominator inconsistency and a one-sided aggregation choice.
**Where.** §5.2: "15 of the 25 restructuring artifacts on which a comparison was performed contain at least one." §5.4 and Table 6: "We evaluate 37 translated artifacts from ten applications", of which ten are c2rust controls — i.e. **27** restructuring artifacts, and I count exactly 15 of those 27 as having ≥1 defect in Table 2. The number 25 is not derivable from any table, and the paper never says which two artifacts it excludes or why. Separately, §5.1 and Table 4(a) report macro-averaged precision only: "Each application row averages its available outputs, and the overall value gives all ten applications equal weight… The pair count… is not used to weight this average."

**Why it matters to me.** A headline ratio whose denominator cannot be reconstructed from the paper's own tables is the single thing I check first, and 25-vs-27 fails it. On the aggregation: the choice is disclosed, which I credit, but it is the choice that flatters the result. Reconstructing a pair-weighted (micro) precision from Table 4(a)'s per-application values and pair counts gives roughly 0.87, about six points below the reported 0.938, because the worst application (tulip, 0.554) carries the second-largest pair count (852). Reporting only the aggregate that hides your worst case, when both are one line of arithmetic apart, is not acceptable at this venue.

**What would resolve it.** Derive the 25 explicitly or correct it to 27; report macro *and* micro precision side by side in Table 4(a), and state which one the abstract quotes.

### W4 (major). No repetitions, no variance, no statistics anywhere — and the one comparative claim runs a different protocol from the table it appears in.
**Where.** §5.4: "The standard campaign runs for one hour"; §Discussion: "Each reported campaign is one fixed-budget observation of one frozen artifact, not an estimate of expected fuzzer performance." The Tulip seed experiment (§5.4) compares "24.0–34.7% region coverage" against "90.2–93.2%" with "a length-matched random control on c2rust reaches only 38.7%" — one run per arm. Table 6's caption: "Tulip reports the deterministic seed-refined campaign described in RQ4."

**Why it matters to me.** One-pass evaluation of a stochastic process is the single most common reliability defect I find when auditing code-evaluation work, and this paper commits it throughout: every number in Table 6, including the headline "8.3–100%" range, is n=1. The disclosure in Threats is honest but does not make the numbers reproducible or the range interpretable. The Tulip case compounds it: one of ten applications gets a hand-chosen option set ("we… sample every option from the fixed set {1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20}"), a 600-second budget instead of an hour, and its *seeded* numbers occupy the main reach table, while the other nine applications get unseeded one-hour campaigns. That makes the headline range a mixture of two protocols. It also sits awkwardly against the paper's own methodological commitment elsewhere ("we do not manually repair its proposals before running a campaign", §5.2): the option set is a manual, subject-specific intervention chosen after observing that unconstrained bytes fail the guard.

**What would resolve it.** Repeat each campaign at least three times with different fuzzer seeds and report mean and spread for at least the cells you draw conclusions from (all the low-reach cells in §5.4, and both Tulip arms); report the unseeded Tulip numbers in Table 6 with the seeded campaign as a clearly separated experiment, or derive the seed values automatically from the guards the planner already parses and apply the same procedure to every subject.

### W5 (major). The negative control does not exercise the machinery that is actually at risk — and its own funnel is not reported.
**Where.** §4.3: "C2Rust retains source structure and C-shaped interfaces… These artifacts therefore exercise the same harness machinery on a structurally faithful representation."

**Why it matters to me.** The paper's own account of the hard problem is adapting *reshaped* interfaces: "a C pointer–length pair and a Rust slice must encode the same logical buffer without silently narrowing the represented input domain" (§1); a "nullable pointer becoming an `Option`" (§4.2). The c2rust control, by construction, presents C-shaped interfaces and therefore never exercises the pointer→slice or nullable→`Option` bridges. So a harness-induced false positive that arises *only* in the adaptation path — exactly the failure mode §1 says is most dangerous — is invisible to this control. The control qualifies the decoder and the ladder; it does not qualify the bridge. Separately, the funnel in Fig. 2 is restricted to "the five restructuring systems", so the control's attrition is never shown, even though (per the archived summaries) it is the paper's cleanest result: 0 of 8,962 adjudicated control candidates reach the confirmed stage.

**What would resolve it.** (i) Add a reshaped-interface control: hand-construct or mechanically generate a small set of *semantics-preserving* pointer→slice / nullable→`Option` rewrites of c2rust output, and show the pipeline reports no confirmed divergence on them. (ii) Add the control row to Fig. 2 and report the 0/8,962 number in §5.2. (iii) A trivial addition: the artifact-level 0/10 vs 15/27 contingency deserves a significance test; you currently assert "This markedly lower observed incidence supports the qualified-negative-control role" with no test at all.

### W6 (major). RQ5 is positive-only, on a corpus your own tool defined, with no false-positive measurement and no adapter sanity check.
**Where.** §5.5: "Because the corpus was discovered by our validator, this RQ measures applicability and positive-case sensitivity on these known defects, not general recall, precision, or relative bug-finding ability"; Table 7; "adapters only package the existing pair and realize its original input contract in the artifact's documented format."

**Why it matters to me.** The scoping sentence is correct and I credit it, but scoping a comparison honestly does not make it informative. Three gaps. (a) No negative side: running FLOURINE and RustAssure on the ten c2rust controls would cost almost nothing and would tell the reader whether "14 of 16" comes with a flood of spurious differences. As written, the baselines are scored only where they can lose. (b) No adapter validation: the adapters are written by the authors, for tools whose failure rates in Table 7 are very high (FLOURINE: 14 A-fail, 3 unsupported; RustAssure: 22 C-fail out of 34 accepted). I cannot distinguish "the released artifact is brittle" from "our adapter mis-packages the pair" without a positive control — e.g., reproducing detection on defects each baseline's own paper reports. (c) No characterization of *which* defects each baseline completed on. The completed subsets (16 and 11) are not random samples of the 36; if they skew toward one taxonomy family, the conditional rates mean something quite different. The two named FLOURINE misses (C16, S17) are given without a mechanism.

**What would resolve it.** Add the control run; add an adapter positive control; report per-family completion and detection for both baselines, and say what the misses have in common.

### W7 (moderate). Discovery and taxonomy are done by the same team with no independent coding and no agreement statistic.
**Where.** §Discussion, Bug and mechanism coding: "The same researchers discovered and categorized the defects. We separate mechanism from symptom, preserve per-instance evidence, and count a defect by its source-level faulty rewrite." §5.1 Panel (b): "we manually label every implemented one-to-one correspondence by inspecting the C and Rust bodies, types, calls, and provenance."

**Why it matters to me.** Every headline in this paper rests on manual labels: the 36 promotions, the seven families, and the 161-pair alignment ground truth in Table 4(b). I do not doubt the authors' care, but the standard for hand-labeled evidence at this scale is two independent annotators, a written rubric, conflict resolution, and a reported agreement coefficient. None of that is present — not the number of annotators, not the rubric, not a kappa. This is the same standard I apply to my own large-scale manual labeling and I cannot waive it here, particularly for Table 4(b), where the manually constructed ground truth is what the matcher is then scored against.

**What would resolve it.** State the labeling protocol; have a second annotator independently re-code a random sample of the 36 promotions and of the 161 correspondence labels; report Cohen's kappa and how disagreements were resolved.

### W8 (moderate). The reproducibility payload is incomplete in exactly the places the adjudication depends on.
**Where.** §4.6: "The C frontend uses libclang and recorded compilation commands; the Rust frontend uses a pinned rust-analyzer release." §4.5: "C-only replay uses ASan and full UBSan"; Table 3 footnote: "Recoverable minimal-UBSan noise filter; Clean C uses full UBSan."

**Why it matters to me.** "Full UBSan" is not a specification. The entire adjudication — the boundary between a translation defect and a reference-associated candidate, and therefore the 36 — is defined by which checks were enabled, which were recoverable, and which were suppressed. The paper does not list the check set for either configuration, nor the clang/LLVM version, nor rustc/cargo-fuzz/libFuzzer versions, nor the pinned rust-analyzer release, nor the hardware, nor the per-side timeout values, nor the total compute consumed by 37 one-hour campaigns plus 14,470 four-mode confirmation replays. It also does not identify the version or commit of each of the six translators or of the ten C applications, even though §Subjects says "we… freeze its source hash before validation" — freeze it and then *print* it. Nor is any cost reported, in CPU-hours or otherwise.

**What would resolve it.** A short, explicit environment and configuration subsection: exact sanitizer flag sets for each of the three replay configurations, all tool and toolchain versions, translator and subject commits, timeout values, hardware, and total compute. This costs half a page and removes an entire class of reviewer doubt.

### W9 (moderate). "0" cells are not joined to reach, so the reader cannot tell which zeros are informative.
**Where.** Table 2 defines "0" as "no confirmed divergence was found at the recorded budget", and §5.2 reports "this occurs in all ten c2rust controls and ten artifacts produced by the restructuring tools." Table 6 shows lodepng × CROWN at 21.0% function / 10.1% region reach and lodepng × c2rust at 22.9% / 12.6%.

**Why it matters to me.** A "0" at ~10% region reach and a "0" at 100% region reach (qsort) are not the same evidence, and the paper's own §5.4 explains at length why. Presenting them with the same symbol in the headline matrix, in a paper whose core message is that acceptance signals must state their domain, is inconsistent with the paper's own standard.

**What would resolve it.** Annotate each "0" in Table 2 with that cell's region reach (or a bounded-evidence tier), so the matrix carries its own qualification.

### W10 (moderate). A normative choice drives 7 of 36 defects and is never defended.
**Where.** Table 5: "Byte-string domain narrowing… 7… Panic or rejection of byte strings accepted by C". §1 states the position: filtering "arbitrary C byte strings… through a Rust string representation" would hide "exactly the domain narrowing that validation should detect."

**Why it matters to me.** Nineteen percent of the headline count depends on the position that a translated function whose Rust signature takes a UTF-8 string, and which therefore rejects non-UTF-8 input its C original accepted, has a *defect* rather than a deliberately narrowed interface contract that callers must now satisfy. That is a reasonable position — it is probably the right one for a migration setting — but it is a position, and the paper asserts it rather than arguing it. It also interacts with W2: four of these seven are the same `to_str().unwrap()` idiom.

**What would resolve it.** One paragraph defending the choice against the alternative reading (e.g., that the C caller domain is the contract being migrated, with evidence that the translated program is deployed as a drop-in replacement), and a sensitivity note stating what the headline becomes if these are reclassified as interface changes.

### W11 (minor, but a framing issue). The matcher is fronted as a contribution while contributing zero net defects on this corpus.
**Where.** §1 lists "name-independent correspondence recovery" as a pillar of the second contribution; §5.1 and Table 8 then report that name equality loses only the renamed qsort public boundary, and "a name-preserved internal boundary still exposes the same unique defect." So on this corpus, replacing the matcher with name equality would change the defect count by zero.

**Why it matters.** I actually like that you report this — it is the honest negative result I look for. But then say it plainly in §5.1 and in the contributions, rather than leaving the reader to assemble it from an ablation row. The matcher's value here is boundary coverage and the ability to validate the program-level contract, not defect yield; claim that.

### W12 (minor). No responsible-disclosure statement.
The paper reports 36 defects in published artifacts of five research systems, 20 of them attributed to one system. There is no statement of whether the authors notified the tool authors, nor of which artifact version each defect was found in. For an FSE submission this is expected, and it also protects the paper's fairness claims.

## 5. Detailed comments per section

**§1 Introduction.** The four obligations are the right decomposition and the qsort walk-through earns its space. Two content issues. (a) The contribution list claims "An empirical characterization of translation defects across six systems and ten applications," but the characterization is dominated by one artifact (10/36) and two applications (21/36); as written the claim promises breadth the corpus does not have. (b) The abstract's "The study confirms 36 distinct translation defects grouped into seven recurring mechanisms" needs the second granularity from W2; "distinct" is doing work here that the counting unit does not support.

**§2 Motivating Study.** Table 1's rows are the right rows. One gap: the row "Rust-only crash or panic → No signal" is the paper's central empirical claim about the 21 semantic defects, and it is asserted for three examples and then generalized in §5.2 ("Rust-only crash fuzzing has no failure signal for their normally terminating executions"). You *ran* Rust-only fuzzing — it is your discovery stage (§4.4) — so you can convert this from an argument into a measurement: report, for each of the 21, that the Rust-only discovery campaign produced no termination artifact attributable to it at the stated budget. That is your headline and it is currently the only major claim in the paper that is reasoned rather than measured.

**§3 Related Work.** Positioning against FLOURINE / VERT / RustAssure and against cozy / DIFFER / C2Rust cross-checking is fair and the reason for not using them as harness baselines is correct ("Supplying our generated harnesses to these frameworks would therefore reuse the component being evaluated"). The delta to prior work, however, remains argued rather than measured: the only measured comparison is RQ5, which is positive-only on a corpus your tool defined (W6). The fuzz-driver subsection is honest about the planner being "a deliberately bounded, definition-driven analogue rather than an implementation of UTOPIA, FuzzGen, or RUBICK" — good, but then no usage-based construction is ever tried, so the claim that usage-based methods are "complementary rather than competing" is untested. Consider at least one artifact where project tests exist, to show what the bounded planner leaves on the table.

**§4.1 Function Matching.** The two-sided margin is a clean formulation and the abstention semantics are right. The problem is threshold provenance: §4.1 says RQ1 evaluates "the abstention threshold ε=0.01", §5.1 calls it "the preselected abstention threshold", and §5.2 says "We freeze the automatic matcher configuration before execution" — but nowhere is it said how 0.01 was chosen, on what data, or whether that data overlaps the 31 outputs on which the operating point (78.4% accepted, 0.993 precision) is then reported. If the threshold was selected on the same outputs, that number is optimistic and must be labeled as such.

**§4.2 Harness Planning.** The auditable-plan design is the paper's strongest idea and "the plan records the construction failure rather than inserting a guessed constant" is the right default. Two things are under-specified. (a) The producer bridge is "frozen" to one producer (§5.4 notes quadtree needs "producer–mutator–consumer"), but the paper never states how often a one-producer bridge suffices versus fails — a count over the 4,222 matched boundaries would quantify the planner's ceiling far better than prose. (b) "Output completeness is not an eligibility gate… We therefore report each harness's oracle strength as termination-only, partial nullness, observable state, or plugin-provided structured state" (§4.4) — this distribution is *never reported*. It should be a column or a small table: how many of the 1,662 built harnesses have which oracle strength? A validator whose oracle is termination-only at most boundaries is a different instrument from one with observable-state comparison everywhere, and the reader currently cannot tell which one this is. This is, to me, a surprisingly large omission given §1's insistence that the observation is the crux.

**§4.3 Harness qualification.** See W5. Also, the argument that c2rust is a good control partly because "Laertes, CROWN, and C2SaferRust explicitly take C2Rust-generated Rust as their starting point" cuts both ways: it strengthens the control for those three and says nothing about SACTOR and PtrTrans, whose outputs are the most reshaped and which contribute 5 of the 36.

**§4.4–4.5 Discovery, replay, confirmation.** The design is sound and the ordering ("The first triage question is therefore *which explanation owns the disagreement*; deduplication comes afterward," §3.3) is the correct one. Missing: the clustering step is described as "group candidates by adjudicated verdict, sanitizer class, and top failure site" (§4.6) with no evaluation at all. How many clusters did the 1,931 confirmed records form? How many clusters map to each of the 36 defects, and were any clusters split or merged by hand during triage? Without that, the cluster stage is an unmeasured component sitting on the promotion path.

**§5.1 RQ1.** See W3 (macro vs micro) and the threshold issue above. Also: precision only, no recall. You report "this operating point accepts 78.4% of pairs" — the 21.6% abstained boundaries are a direct loss of validation surface, and the paper measures its consequence at exactly one data point (qsort). What fraction of the 36 defects sit at boundaries that would have been abstained at ε=0.01 had the truth been different? A sensitivity sweep of ε against both accepted-pair precision and downstream boundary count would turn this from an anecdote into a curve.

**§5.2 RQ2.** See W1, W2, W3, W9. One further point: the SACTOR compile-failure analysis ("its topological translation order rejects the recursive dependency cycles in cJSON, quadtree, and lil… These are repeatable limitations of the translation pipeline, rather than compilation errors in our differential harnesses") is a good and fair piece of diagnosis — this is the kind of attribution I want, and it makes the absence of comparable attribution for the fourteen "×" cells elsewhere more noticeable.

**§5.3 RQ3.** The two case studies are excellent. The taxonomy is the weak part: seven families over 36 single-coder labels (W7), with families confounded with tools (all six semantic-computation substitutions and all three control-flow failures come from C2SaferRust; all three ownership-state corruptions from CROWN). The sentence "The families also form clear strategy-specific signatures in this corpus" is the paper's most over-reaching claim: with n=36, one artifact supplying 10, and no normalization by opportunity, "clear signature" is not supported. "Consistent with" would be defensible; "clear" is not.

**§5.4 RQ4.** The best-analyzed section in the paper. Beyond W4: the reported range "8.3–100% of functions and 2.8–100% of regions" is close to uninformative as a summary statistic given that the four causes you identify explain the spread; consider reporting the distribution conditioned on your own four categories instead of a min–max. Also, "we retain such code because it is part of the artifact being validated, but do not compare raw region counts across translations" is the right rule, yet the abstract-level range does exactly that comparison implicitly.

**§5.5 RQ5.** See W6. The VERT finding is reported with the right epistemic care and I would keep it. One addition: FLOURINE's 14 analysis failures and RustAssure's 22 compilation failures are themselves a finding about the reusability of released validation artifacts — worth one sentence of characterization (what failed, and was it the artifact, the toolchain, or the input shape?) rather than only a funnel column.

**§5.6 Component Analysis.** The rows are the right ablations, but the evidence sets are tiny and, crucially, *unexplained*: "On the fixed nine-defect replay set" — why nine, and how were they chosen? From the manifest these are the nine defects for which the observation matrix happened to have been run, across six cells; that is a convenience sample, and the paper should say so, because "normalized function state recovers all nine defects, whereas return values recover six" is then a statement about a non-random sixth of the corpus. The other rows are n=3, n=1, n=1. Two of the five rows are single witnesses. As it stands this table demonstrates *existence* (a reduced configuration can lose a witness), which is fine, but §5.6's framing — "Observation has the largest measured effect on defect recovery" — is a comparative claim that nine convenience-sampled defects cannot support.

**§6 Discussion / Threats.** The threats are specific and each names a real mitigation, which I appreciate; Selection in particular ("The ten-application corpus was frozen after exploratory work, not sampled prospectively… Earlier notes explicitly allowed replacement subjects") is more candid than most papers manage, and the Alignment-truth paragraph correctly reports that the consequences of PtrTrans's broken lodepng map "remains unmeasured." Missing from Threats: the counting-unit threat (W2) is half-stated but not quantified; single-run fuzzing is disclosed but its consequence for Table 6 is not; and there is no threat covering the fact that the negative control does not exercise the adaptation bridges (W5).

## 6. Questions for the authors

1. **Recall.** Can you inject a stratified set of faulty rewrites — one or two per taxonomy family, drawn from the real defects you already characterized — into the ten c2rust control artifacts and report detection rate per family at the stated budget? If not, what is your argument that the reader should believe this oracle is sensitive to defect classes it did not happen to find?
2. **Triage precision.** Of the 1,931 confirmed divergence records, how many were manually reviewed, how many clusters did they form, and how many clusters were *rejected* at source-level triage (as opposed to merged into one of the 36)? Please give the rejection reasons.
3. **Denominator.** §5.2 says "15 of the 25 restructuring artifacts on which a comparison was performed," but Table 6 contains 37 artifacts of which 27 are restructuring, and I count 15 of those 27 with ≥1 defect in Table 2. Which two artifacts are excluded from the 25, and on what rule?
4. **Aggregation.** What is the pair-weighted (micro) precision for Table 4(a)? My reconstruction from your per-application values and pair counts is about 0.87 versus the reported macro 0.938. Will you report both?
5. **Threshold provenance.** How was ε=0.01 selected, on what data, and does that data overlap the 31 outputs on which you report 78.4% acceptance at 0.993 precision?
6. **Control funnel.** What are the c2rust controls' numbers at each funnel stage? From the archived summaries the controls appear to contribute 8,962 adjudicated records with zero reaching the confirmed stage — if that is right it is the strongest single number in the paper and it is not in the paper. Why is Fig. 2 restricted to the restructuring systems?
7. **Control adequacy.** Since c2rust preserves C-shaped interfaces, the control never exercises the pointer→slice or nullable→`Option` adaptation you identify in §1 as the risky part. What evidence rules out a harness-induced false positive confined to the adaptation path?
8. **Crash-fuzzing claim.** For each of the 21 normally terminating defects, did the Rust-only discovery campaign produce any termination artifact attributable to that defect at the stated budget? Please report this as a measurement rather than as a property argued from the examples in §2.
9. **Counting unit.** How many *distinct translator-level* root causes do the 36 defects correspond to? Specifically, how many of the seven Laertes defects are the same never-called `laertes_init_*` pattern at different sites, and how many of the seven byte-string-narrowing defects are the same `to_str().unwrap()` idiom?
10. **Oracle strength distribution.** §4.4 says you report each harness's oracle strength (termination-only / partial nullness / observable state / plugin structured). What is the distribution over the 1,662 built harnesses, and over the boundaries at which the 36 defects were found?
11. **Variance.** Can you repeat at least the low-reach cells and both Tulip arms with three fuzzer seeds and report spread? And will you report Tulip's unseeded one-hour numbers in Table 6, with the seeded campaign as a separate experiment?
12. **Baseline fairness.** Will you (a) run FLOURINE and RustAssure on the ten c2rust controls to report their false-positive behavior, and (b) validate your adapters by reproducing detections that each baseline's own paper claims? Which taxonomy families do the 16 FLOURINE-completed and 11 RustAssure-completed subsets cover?
13. **Labeling protocol.** How many annotators produced the 36 promotions, the seven family labels, and the 161 manual correspondence labels in Table 4(b)? Was there independent coding, and what is the agreement coefficient?
14. **Configuration.** Please enumerate the exact check sets for "full UBSan" and "minimal UBSan," the per-side timeout values, all toolchain versions, the translator and subject commits whose hashes you say you froze, the hardware, and the total compute consumed.
15. **Normative choice.** Why is a translated function that rejects non-UTF-8 input its C original accepted a defect rather than a narrowed interface contract? What does the headline count become under the alternative classification?
16. **Disclosure.** Were the 36 defects reported to the authors of the five translation systems, and in which artifact versions were they found?

## 7. What this reviewer would need to see to move up one grade

To reach **Weak Accept**, I need the three decisive items closed, and closed with measurements rather than argument: (i) a seeded-defect recall experiment on the control artifacts, stratified by your own taxonomy families, plus the count of confirmed divergences *rejected* at triage, so that the oracle's discriminative power is quantified in both directions (W1, Q1–Q2); (ii) the defect count reported at both granularities — faulty rewrite sites and distinct translator-level root causes — together with a per-artifact distribution that makes the 10-of-36 concentration visible, and per-system counts either normalized by compared artifacts or demoted from a comparison to a description (W2, Q9); and (iii) a reconstructible denominator for the artifact-level ratio plus macro *and* micro precision in Table 4(a) (W3, Q3–Q4). To reach **Accept**, add repeated campaigns with reported spread and a uniform protocol in Table 6 (W4), the control funnel and a reshaped-interface control (W5), the two-sided baseline comparison with adapter validation (W6), an independent second coder with an agreement statistic (W7), and the full configuration and compute disclosure (W8). None of this requires new machinery — it requires running the instrument you already built against itself and printing the numbers you already have.
